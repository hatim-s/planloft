# Authoring guidance — research

Author Markdown only. Never hand-author HTML or generated presentation markup.

Write an evidence-led research note that lets a reader trace claims, uncertainty, and
next steps.

Use this structure:

## Research question
Define the question, intended decision, scope, and exclusions.

## Summary of findings
Give the most decision-relevant findings first. State confidence for each major finding.

## Evidence
Organize evidence by theme rather than source chronology. Link primary sources, note
dates, and distinguish direct evidence from inference. Use tables for comparisons or
datasets.

## Gaps and limitations
Identify missing data, conflicting evidence, sampling limits, and assumptions that
could change the conclusion.

## Implications and next steps
Explain what the evidence supports now and what should be investigated or tested next.

## Sources
List sources with descriptive labels and enough context to judge relevance.

Rules:

- Prefer primary sources and quantify claims when the evidence permits it.
- Never turn uncertainty into false precision.
- Keep quotations short and attribute them directly.
- Keep presentation color-free so the rendered document works in light and dark themes.
